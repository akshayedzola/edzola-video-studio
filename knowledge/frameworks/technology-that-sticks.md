# Technology That Sticks (Field Essays)

**Status:** edzola_opinion
**Sources:** the seven published Field Essays in [`../source-texts/technology-that-sticks/`](../source-texts/technology-that-sticks/) and the EdZola quote cards.

The most video-ready body of EdZola thinking. Each of the seven essays maps naturally to one 4–7 minute explainer.

## Core ideas

- **Mission-rich, systems-poor.** Nonprofits are rich in purpose and short on process clarity and ownership. The gap is rarely the tool.
- **Technology has three jobs. Do them in order:** (1) Infrastructure → (2) Accelerator → (3) Insight engine. Skipping to insight on top of no infrastructure is why dashboards disappoint.
- **AI after the operating system:** process clarity → infrastructure → acceleration → AI.
- **Five readiness conditions** start with leadership buy-in, process clarity and realistic sequencing. Use them to decide whether a partner is ready.
- **Five delivery instincts**, e.g. clarity before code, specificity before commitment, documentation before memory.
- **Blocker protocol.** What to do when a project hits a wall.
- **Stop rebuilding the same system.** Sector patterns repeat across programmes; reuse them.

## Lines that work on screen (from the quote cards)

- "Automate a confused workflow and you get a confused workflow at scale."
- "Technology accelerates discipline. It can't create it."
- "A free tool that no one knows how to use isn't free."
- "A prototype that shows a mismatch in week two is a gift."
- "People own what they help build. They comply with what they're told."
- "Tools feel like progress. Problems feel like delay."
