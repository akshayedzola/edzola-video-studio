# Youth Development & Employability

> Placement letters are easy. Month-six retention is the real test.

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

Most skilling programmes can tell a funder how many youth were trained and how many got an offer letter. Far fewer know who joined, who was still there at 90 days, and whether the salary was what the offer said. The drop between mobilisation and six-month retention is where your model either works or doesn't, and you can't see it without one record per candidate across the whole journey.

## The data thread: Candidate ID

A Candidate ID is issued at the first screening call and follows the young person through training batch, placement and every follow-up call. Store the Skill India Digital (SIDH) or government candidate ID alongside it where the programme is state- or PMKVY-linked.

## Core entities

- **Candidate**: One young person with Candidate ID, age, education, location, source channel and consent.
- **Batch**: A training cohort with a centre, trade or course, trainer, start and end dates.
- **Assessment**: An aptitude test, module competency check or final certification, with scores linked to the candidate.
- **Employer**: A hiring company with sector, locations, roles, HR contact and MoU status.
- **Job Opening**: A specific role with location, CTC, shift, eligibility and number of vacancies.
- **Placement**: Links a candidate to an opening: offer, joining date, salary and verification proof.
- **Follow-up**: A 30, 90, 180 or 365-day check-in recording employment status, income and reasons for exit.

## Stage: Mobilisation & Selection

**Mobilise youth who'll finish the batch, not just fill it**

Mobilisers under target pressure fill seats with anyone who says yes, and a third of the batch is gone by week three. The real job is finding young people who need the programme, want the kind of work it leads to, and have family support to travel for it. Screening data tells you which channels bring in youth who actually complete and get placed.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the centre manager decide which applicants to select for the next batch and which mobilisation channels to put more time into.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Run outreach** (Mobiliser): Community meetings, college visits, alumni referrals and Instagram or WhatsApp posts, each tagged with a source code.
1. **Eligibility call** (Mobiliser, tele-caller): Check age, education, family income, location and interest in the trade. Explain the course and likely job roles honestly.
1. **Aptitude test** (Centre coordinator): Short numeracy, English and work-readiness test at the centre or on a phone link.
1. **Interview and counselling** (Centre manager): Discuss motivation, willingness to relocate or work shifts, and family concerns. Meet parents for young women if needed.
1. **Select and enrol** (Centre manager): Confirm selection, collect documents, assign to a batch and issue Candidate ID.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L3): WhatsApp chatbot that answers course questions and collects eligibility details in Hindi, Tamil or English. *Needs first:* An approved FAQ and fixed eligibility questions, with a handover to a human caller.
- Know Faster (L3): Weekly analysis of which source channels produce candidates who complete and get placed. *Needs first:* Source channel captured consistently on at least three past batches.
- Build Faster (L1): Draft Instagram and WhatsApp outreach posts in regional languages from a course brief. *Needs first:* Accurate course facts, real salary ranges and approved photos.

*Field note:* Mobiliser targets based on enrolment numbers alone reward filling seats. Tie at least part of the target to batch completion and you'll see very different candidates walk in.


## Stage: Skills Training

**Catch batch dropouts in week two, not at certification**

Youth leave training for reasons you can often fix: a family emergency, a travel cost, a trainer who lost them in module two. By the time the final assessment shows empty seats, it's too late. Daily attendance and module-level competency scores tell the trainer who's slipping while they can still act.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the trainer and centre manager decide which candidates need a call, a stipend top-up or extra support this week.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Daily attendance** (Trainer): Mark attendance each session. Biometric or app-based where the scheme requires it, register otherwise.
1. **Module assessments** (Trainer): Short practical or written check at the end of each module, mapped to NSQF or employer competencies.
1. **Life skills sessions** (Life skills facilitator): Communication, workplace behaviour, financial literacy. Track participation and a short self-reflection.
1. **Absence follow-up** (Counsellor, mobiliser): Call any candidate absent two days running. Log reason and agreed action.
1. **Final certification** (Assessment agency): Sector Skill Council or third-party assessment. Record pass, fail and certificate number.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Generate practice questions and role-play scenarios for each NSQF module in the local language. *Needs first:* Approved course content and the module's learning outcomes.
- Work Smarter (L2): AI mock-interview practice where candidates answer common questions by voice and get feedback. *Needs first:* A private deployment, a set of role-specific questions, and trainer-written feedback guidelines.
- Know Faster (L3): Weekly batch health summary for the centre manager, combining attendance, scores and dropout reasons. *Needs first:* Attendance and module scores entered on time for all active batches.

*Field note:* Attendance fraud shows up when payouts depend on it: proxy signatures, batches that stay 'full' with no one in the room. Surprise visits and photo-stamped attendance catch it faster than audits.


## Stage: Job Readiness & Placement

**Match candidates to real openings, then verify they joined**

An offer letter isn't a placement. Candidates turn down jobs when they learn the shift timing, the commute, or that the CTC includes deductions nobody explained. Employers stop hiring from you after two bad batches. Good placement data tracks the gap between offer, joining and first salary slip.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the placement officer decide which candidates to put forward for which openings, and which employers to prioritise for the next batch.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Demand mapping** (Employer relations lead): Meet employers, log openings with role, location, CTC, take-home, shift and eligibility.
1. **CV and mock interviews** (Trainer, placement officer): Build a one-page CV with each candidate. Run at least two mock interviews.
1. **Shortlist and match** (Placement officer): Match candidates to openings on trade, location preference, relocation willingness and scores.
1. **Hiring drive** (Placement officer, employer HR): Employer interviews at the centre or online. Log interview outcome for each candidate.
1. **Offer and joining** (Placement officer): Record offer details, confirm joining on the day and collect offer letter or first salary slip as proof.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Work Smarter (L2): Suggest candidate-to-opening matches using trade, location, shift preference and assessment scores. *Needs first:* Opening and candidate fields structured, with location and shift captured consistently.
- Build Faster (L2): Draft a one-page CV from the candidate's profile, training record and a short voice note about themselves. *Needs first:* Accurate training and certification data in the candidate record.
- Collect Faster (L2): Read salary slips and offer letters to extract CTC, take-home and employer name. *Needs first:* A private deployment, since slips carry personal and bank details.

*Field note:* Placement rates are often counted at offer, but a quarter of offers never turn into a joining. Count joining with proof, or your placement number won't survive a funder audit.


## Stage: Retention & Alumni

**Track who's still employed at 3, 6 and 12 months**

The first three months in a job decide whether it sticks. Candidates quit over a bad supervisor, a hostel far from work, or a first salary that didn't cover rent in the city. Regular check-ins catch this early, and the data tells you which employers, roles and trades lead to careers.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the alumni coordinator decide whom to support now, and helps programme leadership decide which trades and employers to keep investing in.

*How the work flows:*
1. **30-day call** (Alumni coordinator): Check the candidate joined, got paid and is settling in. Log any problem with housing or supervisor.
1. **90, 180 and 365-day follow-up** (Alumni coordinator): Short call or survey on employment status, employer, salary and job satisfaction.
1. **Mentoring support** (Mentor, alumni volunteer): Pair recently placed candidates with an alumnus in the same city or employer.
1. **Re-placement** (Placement officer): Help candidates who've left their job find a new one. Log as re-placement, not new placement.
1. **Alumni community** (Alumni coordinator): WhatsApp groups, meet-ups and alumni speaking to new batches.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L3): WhatsApp or IVRS follow-up that asks four standard questions in the candidate's language and logs the answers. *Needs first:* Consent to follow-up recorded at enrolment and verified phone numbers.
- Know Faster (L3): Quarterly retention analysis by employer, trade and centre, with plain-language findings. *Needs first:* Follow-up coverage above 70% for the cohorts in question.
- Work Smarter (L2): Group exit reasons from open-text follow-up notes into themes for the employer relations team. *Needs first:* At least 50 recorded exit notes in a private deployment.

*Field note:* Most programmes lose contact after the first job change because the candidate's number changes too. Ask for a second contact, such as a parent or friend, at enrolment.
