# Livelihoods & Economic Empowerment

> From wealth ranking to graduation, know which households actually moved.

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

A graduation programme runs 18 to 36 months, and the household that gets a goat in month six is a different household by month 24. Unless you can see its savings, income and assets on one timeline, you can't tell a thriving enterprise from a sold-off asset. Households are what move out of poverty, so households are what the data has to follow.

## The data thread: Household ID

One Household ID is created at the wealth ranking and carries through SHG membership, training, asset transfer, every coaching visit and the graduation survey. The woman participant is linked as the primary member, with her SHG and NRLM or VSLA group ID stored alongside.

## Core entities

- **Household**: The unit of targeting and graduation: location, members, poverty score, primary woman participant and verification status.
- **Participant**: The individual, usually a woman, enrolled in the programme and linked to her household and group.
- **Group**: An SHG, VSLA or business group with meeting schedule, savings rules, bank account status and federation link.
- **Enterprise**: The livelihood activity: goat rearing, kirana shop, tailoring, vegetable farming, with its start date and asset.
- **Disbursement**: Any seed grant, asset transfer, consumption stipend or loan, with amount, date and channel.
- **Coaching Visit**: One mentor home visit with savings, income, asset status, business health and concerns recorded.
- **Graduation Assessment**: The criteria check at programme end, scored per household against the agreed thresholds.

## Stage: Targeting & Mobilisation

**Reach the ultra-poor households that village lists keep missing**

The poorest households are often left out of village meetings, SHGs and government lists. Participatory wealth ranking with the community, followed by a household verification visit, is how you find them. Get targeting wrong and every later rupee goes to families who'd have been fine without you.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the programme officer decide which households are eligible for the programme and which existing or new group each woman joins.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Village transect and social map** (Community resource person (CRP)): Walk the village with residents. Draw every household on a social map, including those at the edges.
1. **Participatory wealth ranking** (CRP, village members): Villagers sort households into wealth categories using locally agreed criteria. Record the bottom categories.
1. **Poverty score survey** (Field officer): Run PPI or Poverty Stoplight with shortlisted households to confirm their level.
1. **Verification visit** (Programme officer): A second person visits each shortlisted home to check housing, assets and food security against the survey.
1. **Group formation** (CRP, field officer): Link selected women to an existing SHG or VSLA, or form a new group of 10 to 20 members.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Transcribe and structure voice notes from wealth ranking meetings into household categories. *Needs first:* A fixed category list agreed with the community and a private deployment.
- Know Faster (L2): Compare wealth ranking, PPI and verification results to show where targeting methods disagree. *Needs first:* All three completed for most households in a village.
- Build Faster (L1): Adapt the PPI or Stoplight survey into Hindi, Odia or Swahili with local examples. *Needs first:* The validated English version and a list of local asset names.

*Field note:* Village leaders sometimes push relatives onto the list. Run wealth ranking with small groups in different hamlets, and always verify with someone from outside the village.


## Stage: Capacity Building

**Build savings habits and a business plan before the asset**

A goat or a shop given to a woman who hasn't saved, planned or kept accounts is often sold within months. Financial literacy, group savings practice and a realistic business plan are what make the asset stick. The data here shows whether groups are meeting, saving and lending, and whether each woman has a plan she believes in.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the field officer decide which participants are ready for asset transfer and which groups need more hand-holding on savings and bookkeeping.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Group meetings and savings** (CRP, group leader): Weekly meeting with savings collected, internal loans recorded and repayments noted in the group register.
1. **Financial literacy sessions** (Field officer): Modules on budgeting, savings, credit, and avoiding moneylender debt. Track attendance per member.
1. **Enterprise training** (Livelihood trainer): Technical training for the chosen activity: goat care, tailoring, kitchen garden, shopkeeping.
1. **Business plan** (Field officer, participant): Draw up a simple plan with costs, expected income, risks and market for the chosen enterprise.
1. **Readiness check** (Programme officer): Review attendance, savings record and plan. Approve for asset transfer or extend training.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Photograph the group's paper register and extract savings, loans and repayments into the app. *Needs first:* A standard register format used by all groups.
- Build Faster (L2): Turn a participant's spoken business idea into a simple one-page plan with costs and income. *Needs first:* Local price lists for inputs, livestock and market rates.
- Work Smarter (L3): IVRS financial literacy lessons in the local language, with short quizzes. *Needs first:* Content reviewed by trainers and matched to the curriculum.

*Field note:* Group registers often look perfect because the CRP fills them in later. Attend a few meetings unannounced and compare cash in hand with the book.


## Stage: Asset & Enterprise Support

**Track every seed grant and goat from purchase to income**

Asset transfer is where the biggest money moves, and where things go missing: livestock that dies in the first month, grants spent on a wedding, loans taken to repay the moneylender. A clean disbursement record and an asset register show funders where the money went and tell mentors which enterprises need help.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the programme officer decide when to release each tranche of support and which households need an early mentor visit.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Asset procurement** (Livelihood coordinator): Buy livestock or stock with the participant present. Record vendor, price, and animal health or item condition.
1. **Transfer and handover** (Field officer): Hand over the asset or grant at the participant's home. Record date, photo and acknowledgement.
1. **Consumption support** (Programme officer): Monthly stipend through bank transfer or mobile money for households that need it during early months.
1. **Loan and bank linkage** (Field officer, bank sakhi): Link groups to bank loans or CIF. Record loan amount, bank and repayment schedule.
1. **Entitlement linkage** (CRP): Help households access MGNREGA job cards, ration cards, pensions and insurance.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Read bank passbook and mobile money screenshots to confirm stipend receipt. *Needs first:* A private deployment, since passbooks carry account details.
- Work Smarter (L3): Match procurement, handover and payment records to flag gaps before audit. *Needs first:* Consistent household IDs in procurement, handover and payment data.
- Know Faster (L3): Monthly summary of assets transferred and early survival issues for the programme manager. *Needs first:* Handover and first mentor visit data entered on time.

*Field note:* Livestock bought in bulk from one trader often arrives sick. Check animal health at purchase and in the first two weeks, when most deaths happen.


## Stage: Coaching & Graduation

**Coach every household until it meets graduation criteria**

Regular mentor visits are what separate graduation programmes from asset handouts. The mentor sees the goat sold for a medical bill, the shop running out of stock, the savings dropping. Visit data shows who's on track, and a fair graduation assessment tells you whether the household can stand without you.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the mentor decide what to focus on at each visit, and helps the programme officer decide which households have graduated.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Mentor home visit** (Mentor): Visit every fortnight in early months, monthly later. Check asset, savings, income, food security and concerns.
1. **Problem solving** (Mentor, participant): Agree one or two actions: vaccinate livestock, restock shop, repay a group loan, attend a gram sabha.
1. **Track progress** (Mentor): Update savings balance, enterprise income, asset count and food security at each visit.
1. **Graduation assessment** (Programme officer): Score the household against agreed criteria: food security, income sources, savings, assets, children in school.
1. **Federation linkage** (CRP): Link graduated households and groups to village organisations or cluster federations for ongoing support.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Voice-note visit logs transcribed into structured fields: savings, income, asset status, actions. *Needs first:* Visit form fields fixed and a private deployment tested in the local language.
- Work Smarter (L3): Flag households whose savings or income have dropped for two visits running. *Needs first:* At least three visits logged per household with consistent fields.
- Know Faster (L1): Draft cohort graduation reports with income, savings and criteria met, by village and mentor. *Needs first:* Graduation assessments complete and reviewed.

*Field note:* Mentors often mark households as graduated to hit targets, then they slide back within a year. Assess graduation with someone other than the mentor, and follow up 12 months later.
