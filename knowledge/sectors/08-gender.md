# Gender Equality & Women's Empowerment

> Counting what women decide, not only what women receive

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

Most gender programmes can tell a funder how many women attended a session or took a loan. Far fewer can say whether a woman now decides how her income is spent, travels to the bank alone, or got the support she needed after disclosing violence. Tracking one woman from mobilisation to leadership, with her consent and her safety built into the design, is how you find out if the programme shifted power or just delivered services.

## The data thread: Member ID

Each woman gets one member ID at enrolment that links her baseline, session attendance, savings, referrals and endline. Sensitive records like GBV referrals sit behind that ID in a restricted layer, so her journey is complete for the people who support her and invisible to everyone else.

## Core entities

- **Member**: The woman enrolled: age band, household role, education, livelihood, intersecting vulnerabilities, and a restricted contact block with a safe-contact preference.
- **Group / Collective**: The SHG, VSLA, cohort or federation she belongs to, with formation date, meeting cycle, leaders and grading.
- **Session**: Each rights, health, financial literacy or vocational session delivered: module, facilitator, date and attendees.
- **Savings & Loan Ledger**: Contributions, loans, repayments and share-outs at member and group level.
- **Referral**: GBV counselling, legal aid, health or scheme referrals, with status and outcome, held in a restricted layer.
- **Agency Survey**: Baseline, midline and endline measures of decision-making, mobility, control over income and group participation.

## Stage: Mobilisation & Enrolment

**Reaching the women the last programme left out**

The women who show up first to a community meeting are rarely the ones with least voice. Widows, Dalit and Adivasi women, women with disabilities and young brides are often kept home or told the group isn't for them. Selection criteria and the baseline you collect here decide who the programme is really for, and whether you'll be able to show change later.

*Decision it serves:* The field coordinator decides which women to prioritise for enrolment and how to form groups, using vulnerability criteria agreed with the community.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Hold community meetings** (Community mobiliser): Meet in spaces women can reach without permission: anganwadi, temple courtyard, water point. Explain the programme and selection openly.
1. **Door-to-door listing** (Community mobiliser): Visit households to find women who didn't attend. Record basic household details and eligibility flags.
1. **Apply vulnerability criteria** (Field coordinator with community): Rank households on agreed criteria like landlessness, female-headed, disability, early marriage. Share the list for community validation.
1. **Take informed consent** (Community mobiliser): Explain what data is collected, who sees it, and her right to withdraw. Record consent and safe-contact preferences.
1. **Run baseline survey** (Enumerator): Collect socio-economic and agency measures privately, away from husbands and in-laws, in her own language.
1. **Form groups** (Field coordinator): Group 10 to 20 women by proximity and affinity. Record meeting day, place and elected leaders.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Voice-based baseline survey in the local language for women who can't read, answered on the enumerator's phone. *Needs first:* A tested question set with validated translations and a private speech model.
- Work Smarter (L2): Flag likely duplicate enrolments across villages using name, age and household details. *Needs first:* Standardised name spellings and household IDs.
- Know Faster (L1): Summarise baseline results by village and social group for the programme design review. *Needs first:* Clean baseline data with consistent coding.

*Field note:* Baselines get done in the courtyard with the mother-in-law present. The agency measures come back high, the endline shows 'decline', and the programme looks like it failed.


## Stage: Rights & Skills Building

**Sessions women keep coming back to, and why**

A curriculum on rights, bodily autonomy and money only works if women attend enough of it and the facilitator can hold hard conversations. Attendance falls in harvest season and after the first session on violence, when some husbands push back. You need to know who's dropping off and whether it's the calendar, the content or the backlash.

*Decision it serves:* The facilitator and field coordinator decide which women to follow up with, which modules to repeat and whether a group needs extra support after a sensitive session.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Schedule sessions** (Field coordinator): Plan the module sequence around the agricultural and festival calendar, agreed with group leaders.
1. **Facilitate session** (Facilitator or community resource person): Deliver module on rights, health, GBV, financial literacy or vocational skills. Use participatory methods, not lectures.
1. **Record attendance** (Facilitator): Mark attendance by member ID at the session. Note late joiners and women who left early.
1. **Check understanding** (Facilitator): Short oral or picture-based check at the end of key modules, like identifying the helpline or calculating loan interest.
1. **Follow up absentees** (Group leader or facilitator): Visit or call women who missed two sessions. Record reason, especially family objection.
1. **Debrief sensitive sessions** (Facilitator and supervisor): After GBV modules, record disclosures handled, referrals made and any backlash reported.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Adapt session scripts and picture cards into local dialects and examples. *Needs first:* A master curriculum and a glossary reviewed by local facilitators.
- Collect Faster (L1): Read photographed paper attendance registers into digital records. *Needs first:* Standard register format with printed member IDs.
- Know Faster (L3): Monthly pattern analysis of attendance drops by module, season and group. *Needs first:* Six months of consistent attendance with absence reasons.

*Field note:* Attendance data shows women dropping off after the GBV module, but nobody records why. The programme cuts the module rather than working with families on the backlash.


## Stage: Economic & Support Linkages

**Savings, credit and GBV referrals women can actually use**

This is where the programme touches money and safety at once. A woman may be saving in her VSLA, applying for a business loan and seeking legal aid for domestic violence in the same month. Linkage counts look good in reports, but what matters is whether she got the loan in her name, kept control of it, and whether the referral led anywhere.

*Decision it serves:* Group leaders decide on loans, and the GBV focal person and field coordinator decide which referrals need follow-up or escalation.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Run savings meetings** (Group treasurer or VSLA facilitator): Record contributions, loans and repayments in the group ledger each meeting.
1. **Assess enterprise or credit needs** (Livelihoods coordinator): Review business plans, check readiness for microcredit or a bank linkage, confirm loan will be in her name.
1. **Link to schemes** (Field coordinator): Help women apply for NRLM support, widow pension, PM Ujjwala or job cards. Track until the benefit arrives.
1. **Handle GBV referrals** (GBV focal person): Refer to counselling, One Stop Centre, protection officer or legal aid with her consent. Agree a safety plan.
1. **Follow up referrals** (GBV focal person): Check in through her safe contact channel. Record whether support was received and whether she's safe.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Digitise handwritten group ledgers from photos into the member-level savings record. *Needs first:* A standard ledger format and a small labelled set of past pages.
- Build Faster (L2): Pre-fill scheme application forms from member records for the coordinator to verify. *Needs first:* Accurate member data and current scheme formats.
- Know Faster (L3): Quarterly analysis of which scheme applications succeed by block and scheme. *Needs first:* A year of tracked applications with outcomes.

*Field note:* Loans are recorded in the woman's name, but nobody asks who decided how the money was used. The repayment rate looks excellent while control over the enterprise sits with her husband.


## Stage: Graduation & Leadership

**Proving shifts in voice, mobility and control after graduation**

Graduation is when funders ask for impact, and attendance and loan numbers won't answer it. The question is whether women now make decisions they couldn't before, lead their collectives, and stand for panchayat or federation roles. That needs an endline built on the same measures as the baseline, and a way to keep tracking women after the programme leaves.

*Decision it serves:* The programme team decides which groups are ready to graduate and federate, and leadership uses endline data to decide what to scale or change.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Assess graduation readiness** (Field coordinator): Review curriculum completion, savings consistency and group self-management against agreed criteria.
1. **Run endline survey** (Enumerator): Repeat baseline agency measures privately. Add questions on leadership roles and collective action.
1. **Certify and graduate** (Programme manager): Hold a graduation event, issue certificates and hand over group records to elected leaders.
1. **Form federations** (Field coordinator with group leaders): Link graduated groups into village organisations or cluster federations. Record leaders and meeting cycles.
1. **Track leadership over time** (Federation leaders and M&E officer): Record women taking panchayat, federation or committee roles, and collective actions taken, at 6 and 12 months.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Know Faster (L2): Run disaggregated baseline-endline comparison and draft the outcome narrative for the donor report. *Needs first:* Paired baseline and endline data with consistent coding.
- Collect Faster (L2): Code open-ended endline responses on collective action into themes. *Needs first:* A codebook built from a sample of responses by the team.
- Build Faster (L2): Draft federation meeting minutes and resolutions from voice notes in the local language. *Needs first:* Consent from the federation and a private speech model.

*Field note:* Endline survey questions get reworded for 'clarity' and no longer match the baseline. The agency data you most need can't be compared, and the report falls back on attendance.
