# Disability & Inclusion

> Count functioning, remove barriers, track inclusion. Nothing about us without us.

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

Persons with disabilities are undercounted, then under-served because they were undercounted. A rights-based programme measures functioning and barriers rather than diagnoses, and it measures whether someone ends up in a classroom, a job or a community role on their own terms. That only works if the person's own goals sit at the centre of the record, and if persons with disabilities help design what gets collected.

## The data thread: Person ID

One Person ID links the Washington Group screen, the functional assessment, the rehab or education plan, every therapy session and device, and the placement and retention record. The UDID number is stored as a linked field when the person has one and chooses to share it.

## Core entities

- **Person**: The person with a disability, their own stated goals, communication preference and consent.
- **Functional Screening**: Washington Group Short Set or Child Functioning Module responses by domain.
- **Assessment**: Functional and medical assessment, disability type under RPwD Act, percentage, certificate and UDID status.
- **Individual Plan**: IEP or IRP: goals set with the person and family, services, devices, review dates.
- **Service / Therapy Session**: Physio, speech, occupational therapy, skilling or caregiver session with goal progress.
- **Assistive Device**: Device prescribed, fitted, trained on and still in use, with maintenance needs.
- **Placement**: School, job or community placement, accommodations provided and retention checks.

## Stage: Identification & Screening

**Finding the persons with disabilities the census never counted**

National figures put disability at about 2% in India; the Washington Group questions usually find several times that. The gap is people with psychosocial, intellectual and less visible disabilities, and children kept at home. Screening in homes and schools is where they first show up in any system.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the CBID coordinator decide who needs a functional and medical assessment, which households to prioritise, and which communities are being missed.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Run community awareness** (CBR worker / DPO member): Sessions led with local disabled persons' organisations on rights, entitlements and what the programme offers.
1. **Screen door to door** (CBR worker): Washington Group Short Set with adults, asked in the local language after consent, with the person answering where possible.
1. **Screen in schools** (Special educator / teacher): Child Functioning Module for children, with parents and teachers, flagging those out of school too.
1. **Record barriers, not just difficulties** (CBR worker): Note physical, attitudinal and communication barriers at home and in the community.
1. **Refer for assessment** (CBID coordinator): Book functional and medical assessment camps or referrals to district hospitals and DDRCs.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Screening form that works with voice, sign-language video prompts and large text. *Needs first:* Validated WG translations and sign-language recordings by Deaf signers.
- Know Faster (L3): Monthly view of who's missing: identification gaps by domain, gender and age against expected prevalence. *Needs first:* Clean WG data with consistent cutoffs.
- Build Faster (L1): Easy-read and local-language versions of awareness material. *Needs first:* Approved source content and input from persons with intellectual disabilities.

*Field note:* Families often answer for adults with disabilities who could answer for themselves. Ask the person first. Proxy data undercounts psychosocial and communication difficulties.


## Stage: Assessment & Planning

**UDID, a functional assessment, and a plan the person wrote**

Without a disability certificate and UDID card, most entitlements stay out of reach: pensions, scholarships, ADIP devices, reservations. The assessment has to measure what the person can do and what gets in the way. The plan that follows must be built with them, around goals they chose.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the rehab team decide which services, devices and accommodations each person gets, and helps the coordinator see who's stuck in the UDID process.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Functional assessment** (Physio / OT / speech therapist): Assess mobility, self-care, communication and participation using ICF-based tools, with the person present and leading.
1. **Medical assessment and certificate** (Medical board / district hospital): Certification of disability type and percentage under the RPwD Act's 21 disabilities.
1. **Facilitate UDID** (Field coordinator): Help apply on the UDID portal, track status, follow up with the medical authority.
1. **Build the individual plan** (Rehab team with person and family): IEP for children or IRP for adults: goals chosen by the person, services, timelines.
1. **Prescribe devices** (Rehab professional): Prescribe wheelchairs, hearing aids, low-vision aids or AAC based on need, environment and preference.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Draft the IEP or IRP from assessment data and the person's goals, in plain language and easy-read. *Needs first:* Structured assessment fields and the person's goals recorded in their words.
- Work Smarter (L2): Match each person to schemes and entitlements they qualify for from their certificate and profile. *Needs first:* An up-to-date rules table of central, state and local schemes.
- Know Faster (L3): Track UDID bottlenecks by district and disability type. *Needs first:* Application and issue dates for every case.

*Field note:* Plans often list what therapists want to deliver rather than what the person wants to do. When the plan says 'improve gait' and the person said 'get to the market alone', you've lost them.


## Stage: Therapy & Skilling

**Therapy, devices and skills measured against the person's goals**

Therapy works when it's regular and tied to what matters in daily life. Devices are often handed out and abandoned within months because nobody checked fit, training or terrain. Skilling and inclusive education need accommodations built in, and caregivers need support too.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the therapist and rehab coordinator decide whose plan needs adjusting, whose device isn't working for them, and which caregivers need more training.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Deliver therapy** (Physio / OT / speech therapist): Sessions at the centre, at home or via tele-rehab; goal progress rated each session.
1. **Fit and train on devices** (Rehab technician): Fit the device, train the person and caregiver, check it works in their home and route.
1. **Run skilling or inclusive education** (Trainer / special educator): Vocational training or school support with accommodations: sign interpreters, screen readers, ramps, extra time.
1. **Train caregivers** (Therapist / CBR worker): Home programme, positioning, communication strategies; caregiver's own wellbeing noted.
1. **Review goals** (Rehab team with person): Monthly review of progress against plan goals, adjusting services.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Generate home exercise programmes with illustrated, easy-read instructions. *Needs first:* Therapist-approved exercise library and the person's plan.
- Collect Faster (L2): WhatsApp voice notes from caregivers on progress and device issues, transcribed to fields. *Needs first:* Consent, a private transcription service, and a fixed issue list.
- Work Smarter (L2): Assistive tech: AI captioning, screen readers and text-to-speech during training. *Needs first:* Tested tools that work in the trainee's language.

*Field note:* Distribution camps count wheelchairs given. Nobody goes back to see the ones rusting in a corner because the chair didn't fit the doorway. Track use, not handover.


## Stage: Inclusion & Follow-up

**Placed is a start. Still there after a year is inclusion.**

Enrolment and placement numbers are easy. Retention is where the barriers show: an inaccessible toilet, a teacher who won't adapt, a manager who never made the accommodation. Follow-up with the person, the school and the employer is where inclusion is kept or lost.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the inclusion coordinator decide which placements need an intervention now, which schools or employers need sensitisation or accessibility fixes, and gives funders honest retention data.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Place in school or job** (Placement officer / inclusive education coordinator): Match with schools or employers ready to provide reasonable accommodation, under the RPwD Act and Samagra Shiksha.
1. **Audit accessibility** (Access auditor (often a person with disability)): Audit the school or workplace for physical, digital and information accessibility; share a fix list.
1. **Sensitise teachers and employers** (Trainer / DPO): Disability awareness and accommodation training, co-led by persons with disabilities.
1. **Track retention** (Placement officer): Check-ins at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months with the person and, with consent, the employer or teacher.
1. **Resolve barriers** (Inclusion coordinator): Escalate accommodation denials, broker fixes, or support a move if the person wants one.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Turn accessibility audit findings into a prioritised fix list with cost estimates for each institution. *Needs first:* Structured audit data and a standards reference (e.g. Harmonised Guidelines).
- Know Faster (L3): Analyse which accommodations and employers link to 12-month retention. *Needs first:* Complete retention and accommodation data over at least a year.
- Work Smarter (L3): Match persons to jobs based on skills, accommodations needed and the employer's audit record. *Needs first:* Clean skills, preference and employer accessibility data.

*Field note:* Employers agree to accommodations at placement and quietly forget them by month three. The 90-day check-in with the person, alone, is where you hear about it.
