# Human Rights & Access to Justice

> From grievance to enforced remedy, with every deadline tracked

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

A legal aid case can run for years across a paralegal, two lawyers, three forums and a dozen adjournments. When the file lives in a lawyer's diary, limitation periods lapse, clients lose track of their own matters, and a decree gets won but never enforced. A structured case record protects the client, meets the duty of confidentiality, and turns individual cases into evidence for systemic advocacy.

## The data thread: Case ID

One case ID is opened at intake and follows the matter through advice, documentation, every hearing and enforcement of the remedy. It links to the court's CNR or file number once filed, so the client's history and the court record stay connected.

## Core entities

- **Client**: The person seeking help: eligibility basis, contact preferences, language, and a restricted identity block covered by legal privilege.
- **Case**: The legal matter: issue type, forum, stage, strategy, limitation date, assigned paralegal and advocate, status.
- **Opposite Party**: The respondent, whether a government department, employer, landlord or individual, used for conflict checks and pattern analysis.
- **Document & Evidence**: Identity proofs, notices, RTI replies, FIR copies, affidavits and evidence, with date received and chain of custody.
- **Hearing**: Each date before a court, tribunal, Lok Adalat or authority: purpose, outcome, next date and who appeared.
- **Remedy**: The order, settlement or administrative decision obtained, and whether it has been enforced.

## Stage: Awareness & Intake

**Legal literacy camps that turn into registered grievances**

Most people who need legal help never call it a legal problem. A denied pension, wages withheld, a demolition notice or a missing FIR only becomes a case when a paralegal hears it at a camp and writes it down. Intake decides whether a matter gets triaged on time, whether the limitation clock was noticed, and whether you took a conflicted case by accident.

*Decision it serves:* The intake coordinator decides whether a grievance is eligible, urgent and within mandate, and who takes it forward.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Run legal literacy camp or clinic** (Paralegal or community legal worker): Hold sessions on rights in the local language. Record attendance and grievances raised on the spot.
1. **Register grievance** (Paralegal or helpline staff): Record the client's account, issue type and opposite party. Explain confidentiality and get consent to proceed.
1. **Screen eligibility** (Intake coordinator): Check eligibility for free legal aid, such as Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, or organisational criteria.
1. **Check urgency and limitation** (Intake coordinator): Flag bail, eviction, custody or imminent limitation. These go to a lawyer the same day.
1. **Run conflict check** (Intake coordinator): Search opposite party against existing clients before accepting. Record the result.
1. **Assign or refer** (Intake coordinator): Assign to a paralegal or advocate, or refer to DLSA, another organisation or a government office.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Transcribe and structure a client's voice-note account into intake fields in the local language. *Needs first:* A private speech deployment and a fixed intake schema.
- Work Smarter (L2): Suggest issue type and likely forum from the grievance description. *Needs first:* A labelled set of past intakes with issue types agreed by lawyers.
- Know Faster (L3): Weekly summary of grievance trends by issue and district for the programme lead. *Needs first:* Consistent issue coding and geography across intakes.

*Field note:* Grievances from camps sit in a paralegal's notebook for weeks. By the time they reach a lawyer, a 30-day appeal window under a welfare scheme has already closed.


## Stage: Case Assessment & Documentation

**Building the evidence file before the first hearing**

Cases are won or lost on the file. A missing RTI reply, an unsigned affidavit or a lost copy of the original notice can sink a strong claim. This stage is where the lawyer weighs the options, the paralegal chases documents, and the client needs to understand what's being filed in their name.

*Decision it serves:* The assigned lawyer decides the legal strategy and forum, based on the facts, evidence gathered and the client's informed choice.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Advise on options** (Advocate): Explain options such as mediation, administrative remedy, litigation, with likely time and risk. Record the client's instructions.
1. **Fact-finding** (Paralegal): Visit site, speak to witnesses, collect documents. Record what was found and where evidence is held.
1. **File RTIs and notices** (Paralegal or advocate): Draft RTI applications, legal notices or representations. Track dates filed and reply deadlines.
1. **Draft pleadings** (Advocate): Prepare petitions, applications or complaints. Get vakalatnama and affidavits signed.
1. **Write strategy note** (Advocate): Record the legal theory, forum, relief sought and key risks, reviewed by a senior lawyer.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Draft RTI applications and legal notices from case facts using the organisation's templates. *Needs first:* Vetted templates and a private deployment.
- Collect Faster (L1): Extract dates, parties and key facts from scanned documents into the case file. *Needs first:* Readable scans and a defined field list.
- Work Smarter (L2): Search past case files and judgments for precedent on similar facts. *Needs first:* An indexed internal case library and verified judgment sources.

*Field note:* Original documents get handed to the lawyer and never logged. Two years later, at the evidence stage, nobody can find the one notice that proves the date.


## Stage: Resolution & Representation

**Every hearing, adjournment and next date on one record**

Once a matter is filed, it's a long run of dates. Adjournments stack up, lawyers change, and a missed hearing can mean a case dismissed for default. Mediation and administrative remedies move faster but need their own tracking, and clients want to know where their case stands without having to travel to the office.

*Decision it serves:* The advocate and legal director decide how to proceed at each stage: settle, press on, appeal or escalate, based on hearing outcomes and client instructions.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Mediate or negotiate** (Paralegal or mediator): Attempt settlement where suitable, through Lok Adalat, community mediation or direct negotiation. Record terms agreed.
1. **Pursue administrative remedy** (Paralegal): File applications with the department or authority. Follow up and escalate to appellate officers.
1. **File litigation or PIL** (Advocate): File in the appropriate forum. Record CNR or file number and first hearing date.
1. **Appear and record hearing** (Advocate): Attend each date. Record purpose, what happened, order passed and next date the same day.
1. **Update client** (Paralegal): Tell the client what happened and what's next, in their language, through their preferred channel.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L3): Pull case status and next dates from eCourts into the case record daily. *Needs first:* CNR numbers recorded correctly for every filed case.
- Build Faster (L2): Summarise court orders into a plain-language update for the client in their language. *Needs first:* Private deployment and a reviewed glossary of legal terms.
- Know Faster (L3): Monthly analysis of adjournment patterns by forum and reason. *Needs first:* Consistent adjournment reason coding over at least six months.

*Field note:* Lawyers update the diary but not the case record. When an advocate leaves, their replacement has a stack of files and no idea which ones have a hearing next week.


## Stage: Follow-up & Systemic Advocacy

**Enforced remedies and case data that change policy**

A favourable order is not the remedy. Compensation goes unpaid, reinstatement orders are ignored, and entitlements stay stuck at the department. Following through on enforcement is where many legal aid wins quietly fail. Aggregated across hundreds of cases, the same records show which departments deny rights routinely, and that's the evidence base for policy change.

*Decision it serves:* The legal director decides which cases need enforcement action and which patterns warrant strategic litigation, a PIL or policy advocacy.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Monitor enforcement** (Paralegal): Check whether the order, settlement or benefit has been implemented. Record date and amount received.
1. **File execution or contempt** (Advocate): Where orders aren't complied with, file execution petition or contempt, and track as a linked matter.
1. **Close case with client feedback** (Paralegal): Close only after remedy is received or the client withdraws. Record the client's feedback on the process.
1. **Aggregate case data** (Research or M&E officer): Analyse patterns by issue, opposite party and region. De-identify before any sharing.
1. **Advocate for change** (Legal director and advocacy team): Use patterns in submissions to authorities, strategic litigation, PILs or public reports.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Know Faster (L3): Quarterly pattern analysis of case outcomes by opposite party, issue and district. *Needs first:* Consistent coding of issues and opposite parties across years of cases.
- Build Faster (L2): Draft advocacy briefs and submissions from de-identified case analytics. *Needs first:* Validated analysis and an agreed advocacy position.
- Work Smarter (L3): Flag closed cases where enforcement hasn't been confirmed. *Needs first:* Remedy status recorded consistently at closure.

*Field note:* Cases get closed when the order comes in. Nobody checks whether the compensation was paid, and the annual report counts a win that the client never felt.
