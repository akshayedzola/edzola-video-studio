# Community Development

> Villages own the plan. The data should prove who was heard.

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

Community projects run on trust, and trust breaks when the gram sabha can't see where the money went or why their priority got dropped. The water tank built in year one only matters if the O&M fund is still collected in year five. Linking the baseline, the plan, the works and the handover in one record is how you show a village, a panchayat and a funder the same truth.

## The data thread: Village ID

Every village gets one Village ID at selection, keyed to its Census or LGD code, and every household, committee, plan item, work and O&M record hangs off it. Households carry their own Household ID under it, so you can check every family was reached, not just the village.

## Core entities

- **Village**: The settlement keyed to its LGD code, with panchayat, hamlets, population, baseline and programme phase.
- **Household**: Every family from the enumeration, with social group, head, members, housing, water and sanitation status.
- **Committee**: A village committee, VWSC, SHG or user group with members, office bearers, meeting records and bank account.
- **Need**: A problem raised in PRA or a meeting, with who raised it, which hamlet and its priority rank.
- **Work**: A physical project: water system, toilet, house, drain, road, with estimate, milestones and status.
- **Fund Entry**: Money in or out: grant, community contribution, scheme funds, expenses and O&M collections.
- **Scheme Linkage**: An application to a government scheme like JJM, SBM, PMAY or MGNREGA, with status and amount sanctioned.

## Stage: Community Entry & Mobilisation

**Enumerate every household, including the hamlet past the river**

The first weeks in a village decide who the project belongs to. If you only meet the sarpanch and the families near the main road, the Dalit hamlet and the landless households get left out of everything that follows. A full household enumeration and a committee that reflects the whole village are the foundation.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the programme coordinator decide whether a village is ready to start, and whether its committee represents every hamlet and social group.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Village selection** (Programme manager): Shortlist villages using Census, JJM and SBM data, then visit to check need and willingness.
1. **Community meetings** (Community mobiliser): Hold meetings in each hamlet, not just the main village. Explain the programme and the community's role.
1. **Household enumeration** (Community mobiliser, local volunteers): Visit every house. Record members, social group, housing, water source and toilet status.
1. **Committee formation** (Community mobiliser): Form a village committee with seats for women and every hamlet and social group, elected in an open meeting.
1. **Baseline survey** (M&E officer): Run the baseline on key indicators for a sample or all households, depending on programme design.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Read handwritten meeting attendance sheets into counts by gender and hamlet. *Needs first:* A standard sheet format with hamlet and gender columns.
- Know Faster (L2): Compare enumeration data with Census, JJM and SBM figures to spot gaps before planning. *Needs first:* Enumeration complete and LGD codes mapped.
- Build Faster (L1): Generate a hamlet-wise household map from GPS points for the committee's use. *Needs first:* GPS captured for most households.

*Field note:* Early meetings called by the sarpanch tend to draw the same families. Hold separate meetings in each hamlet and with women's groups before any committee is formed.


## Stage: Participatory Planning

**Record every priority the gram sabha raised, and what happened**

PRA exercises generate charts and ranked lists that end up rolled in a cupboard. When the village development plan drops a hamlet's top need without saying why, people stop coming to meetings. A needs log that tracks each priority from PRA to plan to budget keeps the process honest.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the village committee and programme coordinator decide which needs go into the development plan and what the community will contribute.

*How the work flows:*
1. **PRA exercises** (Community mobiliser, villagers): Resource maps, seasonal calendars, matrix ranking and transect walks in each hamlet, with women's groups separately.
1. **Needs log** (Community mobiliser): Record each need raised, which hamlet and group raised it, and its rank in that exercise.
1. **Gram sabha prioritisation** (Village committee, gram panchayat): Present the needs log in the gram sabha. Agree priorities and record the decision and attendance.
1. **Village development plan** (Programme engineer, committee): Turn priorities into works with estimates, timelines, funding sources and community contribution.
1. **Contribution agreement** (Village committee): Agree cash, labour or material contribution per household and open a corpus or O&M account.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Transcribe gram sabha recordings in the local language into a draft list of decisions. *Needs first:* Consent to record from the gram sabha and a private deployment.
- Work Smarter (L2): Group raw needs from PRA notes into categories and link duplicates across hamlets. *Needs first:* Needs logged with hamlet and group tags.
- Build Faster (L1): Draft the village development plan document from approved needs, estimates and funding sources. *Needs first:* Needs log complete and estimates prepared by the engineer.

*Field note:* Elite capture often happens between the PRA and the gram sabha. Compare the plan against the needs log by hamlet before sign-off, and ask why any hamlet got nothing.


## Stage: Implementation & Delivery

**Track works, funds and scheme money in one ledger**

Water systems, toilets and houses are built over months, with money from grants, community contributions, MGNREGA and JJM all flowing in at different times. Delays, cost overruns and missing contributions are normal. Without a works tracker tied to a fund ledger, the committee can't show the village where the money went, and social audits get hard.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the programme engineer and village committee decide which works need attention this week and whether to release the next payment.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Work orders** (Programme engineer): Issue work orders to masons or contractors with milestones, materials and payment schedule.
1. **Milestone inspection** (Programme engineer, committee member): Inspect each milestone on site. Record progress, quality issues and photos.
1. **Fund tracking** (Committee treasurer, accountant): Record every rupee in and out: grant tranches, contributions, scheme funds and expenses.
1. **Scheme linkage** (Community mobiliser): Help households and the panchayat apply for JJM, SBM, PMAY or MGNREGA. Track application to sanction.
1. **Trainings and awareness** (Community mobiliser): Hygiene, water safety and O&M training for households and committees. Log attendance.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Read bills, measurement book pages and cash book entries into the fund ledger. *Needs first:* Standard bill formats and a private deployment for financial records.
- Work Smarter (L2): Compare inspection photos across milestones to flag works that look unchanged. *Needs first:* Photos taken from consistent angles at each inspection.
- Know Faster (L3): Monthly works and funds summary per village in plain language for the committee meeting. *Needs first:* Works and fund entries updated before month end.

*Field note:* Community contributions are often recorded as agreed but never fully collected. Track paid vs pledged by household, or the O&M fund will be empty on day one.


## Stage: Handover & Sustainability

**Keep the water flowing five years after handover**

The real test of a community project is whether the pump still works after the NGO leaves. Assets fail when the O&M fund runs dry, the pump operator moves away or the committee stops meeting. Handover records and regular follow-up visits show which villages are holding up and which need a hand before the system breaks down.

*Decision it serves:* Helps the programme manager decide which handed-over villages need a follow-up visit or committee support, and what to change in future handovers.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Handover agreement** (Programme manager, village committee, panchayat): Sign a handover MoU listing assets, O&M roles, fund balance and the panchayat's responsibilities.
1. **O&M fund setup** (Committee treasurer): Agree monthly user fees, open or confirm the bank account and set a collection schedule.
1. **Operator training** (Programme engineer): Train a local pump operator or mason in basic repairs. Record contact and skills.
1. **Follow-up visits** (Community mobiliser): Visit at 6, 12, 24 and 36 months. Check asset function, fund balance and committee meetings.
1. **Endline survey** (M&E officer): Repeat baseline indicators for comparison, using the same households or sample.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L3): IVRS calls to committee treasurers and operators asking whether the system works and the fund balance. *Needs first:* Verified contacts and a short, tested question script in the local language.
- Know Faster (L2): Analyse follow-up data to find what separates villages with working assets from those without. *Needs first:* At least 30 villages with 24 months of follow-up data.
- Build Faster (L1): Draft the endline report comparing baseline and endline, by village and hamlet. *Needs first:* Endline matched to baseline households.

*Field note:* Handover often happens when the grant ends, not when the village is ready. Set handover criteria at the start, such as a fund balance and active committee, and don't hand over until they're met.
