# Media & Journalism

> From the tip to the correction to the policy that changed

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

Newsrooms drown in reach numbers and can't say which stories changed anything. A story that got 2 lakh views and one that got a district collector to act look the same in a Google Analytics export. Linking the tip, the story, the fact-check and what happened afterwards is how a public-interest newsroom proves its value to readers and funders.

## The data thread: Story ID

Each story gets one ID the moment it's pitched. The source tips, reporter assignment, fact-check verdicts, edits, translations, channel metrics, reader responses and any correction or real-world impact all attach to it.

## Core entities

- **Contributor**: A staff or stringer reporter, fact-checker or translator, with beat, district, languages, training completed and payment terms.
- **Story**: A pitched, assigned or published piece: format, beat, geography, editorial status, language versions and publication links.
- **Claim**: A specific checkable statement, with source, date, verdict rating and the evidence behind it.
- **Source**: A person or document the newsroom relies on, with protection level and contact restricted to the assigned reporter and editor.
- **Channel Metric**: Views, shares, watch time, forwards or screening attendance for one story on one channel over a period.
- **Impact Event**: A traceable real-world response to a story: official action, correction by another outlet, policy change, citation in court or parliament.

## Stage: Sourcing & Newsgathering

**Build a stringer network the story budget can count on**

Local reporters in districts that mainstream outlets ignore are the whole point, and the whole risk. If you don't know who covers which block, in what language, and when they were last paid, the story budget is a wish list. Tips and claims come in on WhatsApp all day and most get lost in someone's phone.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the news editor decide which pitches to assign, to whom, and which claims are worth checking today.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Recruit and train reporters** (Bureau chief, trainers): Onboard stringers by district and language. Log training in verification, ethics, safety and mobile video.
1. **Monitor claims** (Monitoring desk): Track viral claims, political speeches, WhatsApp tiplines and official data releases. Log each checkable claim.
1. **Pitch stories** (Reporters): Submit pitches with angle, sources, format and estimated time. Tag beat and district.
1. **Hold story meeting** (News editor, desk): Rank pitches and claims against the story budget. Assign reporter and deadline.
1. **Log sources** (Reporter): Record sources in a restricted contacts log with protection level. Never in shared sheets.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L3): Cluster incoming WhatsApp tips and social claims so the desk sees duplicates as one claim. *Needs first:* A tipline that stores messages in your own system, not a personal phone.
- Know Faster (L2): Transcribe and flag checkable statements in political speeches and press conferences. *Needs first:* A speech model tested on your languages and dialects.
- Work Smarter (L2): Suggest the best-placed reporter for a pitch by district, language and workload. *Needs first:* Current contributor roster and assignment history.

*Field note:* Tips arrive on the editor's personal WhatsApp and die there. When the editor leaves, the newsroom loses its tip history.


## Stage: Production & Verification

**Verdicts that survive a legal notice, in every language**

Verification is the product. A wrong verdict or a missed legal red flag costs more credibility than a year of good work earns. The fact-check record has to show the evidence trail, who approved it and which language versions went out, because someone will challenge it.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the editor and legal reviewer decide whether a story or fact-check is ready to publish, and in which languages.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Report and draft** (Reporter): File copy, video or audio against the Story ID with notes on sources and documents.
1. **Check claims** (Fact-checker): Break the story into claims, verify each with primary evidence, assign a rating using the house methodology.
1. **Edit** (Sub-editor, editor): Line edit, headline, check verdict language matches evidence. Log changes.
1. **Legal review** (Legal reviewer): Flag defamation, contempt, privacy and child identity risks. Clear or return with notes.
1. **Translate and check** (Translators, language editor): Produce language versions, back-check verdict and numbers against the original.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Know Faster (L2): Search the newsroom's past fact-checks for the same or similar claim. *Needs first:* An archive of past checks with consistent claim text and ratings.
- Build Faster (L2): Draft first-pass translations of cleared copy into regional languages. *Needs first:* Locked, legally cleared master copy.
- Collect Faster (L2): Transcribe field interviews and video in local languages for reporters to quote from. *Needs first:* A private transcription tool, since interviews may contain source identities.

*Field note:* Language versions get published without a back-check. The Hindi original says 'misleading', the regional version says 'false', and a legal notice follows.


## Stage: Distribution & Engagement

**Publish where readers are: web, WhatsApp, radio and village screenings**

In much of the audience, the story travels on WhatsApp forwards, community radio and a projector in the panchayat hall. Web analytics see a fraction of that. If you don't log screenings, partner republishing and reader tips against the Story ID, you're counting the wrong audience.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the audience editor decide which channels, partners and formats to push each story through, and which reader tips to follow up.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Package per channel** (Audience team): Cut vertical video, WhatsApp card, audio clip and web version. Tag each with the Story ID.
1. **Publish and syndicate** (Audience editor): Post on web, social, WhatsApp channel. Share with partner outlets and log republication.
1. **Hold community screenings** (Local reporters): Screen stories in villages, log attendance and discussion notes.
1. **Respond to readers** (Engagement desk): Answer comments and tips, route new claims to the monitoring desk.
1. **Record metrics** (Audience analyst): Pull channel metrics weekly into the story record, not separate dashboards.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Cut a cleared story into WhatsApp card, social caption and audio script drafts. *Needs first:* Legally cleared copy and house style guides per channel.
- Know Faster (L3): Weekly analysis of which formats and channels carry which beats. *Needs first:* Channel metrics linked to Story IDs.
- Work Smarter (L2): Draft replies to common reader questions on the tipline for the engagement desk. *Needs first:* A library of approved answers and published checks.

*Field note:* Screenings get reported as a count in a funder report with no link to the story shown. Nobody can tell which stories actually sparked discussion.


## Stage: Impact & Audience Tracking

**Reach versus impact: the story that got the handpump fixed**

Funders ask for impact and newsrooms send pageviews. Impact is a collector's order, a corrected government figure, a claim retracted, a court citing your check. It's rare, slow and has to be logged the week it happens, or it's gone.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the editor-in-chief and revenue team decide which beats and formats to invest in, and what to tell funders and members.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Log impact events** (Reporters, editors): Record any official response, correction, policy change or citation linked to the Story ID, with evidence.
1. **Track corrections** (Standards editor): Log corrections to own stories and corrections by others after our check.
1. **Manage audiences** (Audience team): Track newsletter, WhatsApp channel and member retention by cohort.
1. **Manage donors and members** (Revenue team): Record contributions, renewals and restricted grants against beats.
1. **Report to funders** (Editor-in-chief, MEL): Compile impact log, reach and audience growth into funder reports.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L3): Monitor government orders, assembly questions and news for mentions of your stories or checks. *Needs first:* A list of Story IDs, headlines and key claims to match against.
- Build Faster (L2): Draft funder reports from the impact log, reach data and grant terms. *Needs first:* A verified impact log and clean metrics by beat.
- Know Faster (L3): Analyse retention cohorts to see which beats bring readers who stay. *Needs first:* Subscriber data linked to first story read.

*Field note:* Impact lives in a reporter's memory and gets reconstructed in a panic before the annual funder report. Half of it's lost and the rest is overclaimed.
