# Arts & Culture

> Follow the artist from open call to archive to career

**Status:** edzola_opinion (EdZola MAP Field Guide). Fields and indicators are starting points for a human to adapt.

Arts organisations hold years of applications, jury notes, programmes and photographs, scattered across inboxes and hard drives. When a funder asks what happened to the 2019 cohort, nobody can answer without a week of digging. One artist record, linked from open call to archive, lets you show what support did to practice, audiences and careers.

## The data thread: Artist ID

Each artist, collective or learner gets one ID at their first application. Every jury score, grant, residency, rehearsal log, performance, archive asset and alumni update links to it, whether they were selected or not.

## Core entities

- **Artist**: An individual, collective or learner: discipline, languages, region, practice stage and contact, with applications across years.
- **Open Call**: A call for applications: programme, discipline, eligibility, deadline, jury and selection criteria.
- **Application**: One artist's submission to one call: proposal, work samples, budget and jury scores.
- **Grant or Residency**: The award: amount, tranches, milestones, mentor, venue, dates and reporting obligations.
- **Event**: A performance, exhibition, screening, workshop or school session, with venue, date, audience count and production notes.
- **Archive Asset**: A photo, recording, script, catalogue or report, with rights, credits, date and link to the artist and event.

## Stage: Open Call & Selection

**Open calls that reach beyond the usual metros and networks**

Open calls tend to reward artists who already know how to write proposals in English. If you don't track who applies, from where, in what language and through which channel, you can't tell if your call is open or just open to the same circle. The jury process also has to be fair and seen to be fair.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the programme team and jury decide who is shortlisted and selected, and lets leadership see whether the call is reaching new artists.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Publish the call** (Programme officer): Post in English and regional languages on web, social, WhatsApp groups and through partner arts spaces. Tag each channel.
1. **Receive applications** (Programme officer): Online form with option for audio or video proposals and regional language submissions.
1. **Screen for eligibility** (Programme team): Check completeness and eligibility. Log reasons for any rejection at screening.
1. **Jury review** (Jury members): Score each application on published criteria. Declare conflicts of interest. Record comments.
1. **Shortlist and select** (Jury, programme head): Deliberate on scores, finalise selection, and send outcome with feedback to all applicants.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Transcribe and translate audio or regional-language proposals for jurors who don't share the language. *Needs first:* Applicant consent and a translator available to check.
- Build Faster (L1): Draft the open call in multiple languages from the approved English text. *Needs first:* Final approved call text and eligibility rules.
- Know Faster (L2): Summarise applicant pool patterns across cycles: regions, disciplines, first-timers. *Needs first:* Consistent fields across call cycles.

*Field note:* Applicant data lives in a Google Form per year with different fields each time. After five years, no one can say whether the call is reaching new artists.


## Stage: Creation & Capacity Support

**Residencies, mentorship and rehearsals tracked without policing the process**

Creative work doesn't move in straight lines, and grantees resent reporting that treats a residency like a construction project. You still need to know tranches were released, mentors showed up and projects are alive. Light milestone check-ins do that without turning artists into form-fillers.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the programme officer decide when to release tranches and where a project needs extra mentoring or a timeline change.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Sign the agreement** (Programme officer, artist): Grant or residency agreement with amount, tranches, milestones, dates and reporting format.
1. **Assign mentor and venue** (Programme team): Match mentor by discipline, book studio or rehearsal space, share schedule.
1. **Run workshops and rehearsals** (Mentors, faculty): Log sessions, attendance for ensembles or learners, and brief notes on progress.
1. **Milestone check-ins** (Programme officer, artist): Conversation at agreed points, logged as short notes and any work-in-progress samples.
1. **Release tranches** (Finance, programme officer): Release payment once the milestone is recorded. Log date and amount.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Build Faster (L2): Generate the grant agreement from the selection record and standard terms. *Needs first:* A locked agreement template and approved grant details.
- Collect Faster (L2): Turn voice notes from mentors after sessions into structured session logs. *Needs first:* Mentor consent and a private transcription tool.
- Work Smarter (L3): Surface grantees with no check-in or mentor session in 60 days. *Needs first:* Consistent session and check-in logging.

*Field note:* Milestones get written by the funder in log-frame language. Artists either game them or feel they've failed when the work shifts direction.


## Stage: Presentation & Audience

**Who came, who came back, and who we still don't reach**

Audience numbers from a festival are easy to inflate and hard to interpret. What matters is who you reached for the first time, which school or community sessions happened, and whether audiences return. Ticketing data, free event headcounts and outreach logs have to sit together or you'll only see your paying regulars.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the programme and outreach team decide where to take work next, which venues and formats bring new audiences, and how to price.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Plan the production** (Production manager): Log venue, dates, technical rider, crew and budget per event.
1. **Sell and register** (Box office, outreach team): Ticketing for paid events, registration or headcount for free and community events.
1. **Run school and community sessions** (Outreach facilitators): Log school, grade band, participants and teacher feedback per session.
1. **Count and survey audiences** (Front of house): Headcount at door, short audience survey by QR or paper.
1. **Close the production log** (Production manager): Record actuals, issues, and link photos and recordings.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Know Faster (L2): Read open-text audience survey responses and group them by theme. *Needs first:* Anonymised survey responses and enough volume to find patterns.
- Build Faster (L1): Draft programme notes, press releases and captions in multiple languages from production details. *Needs first:* Artist-approved descriptions and credits.
- Work Smarter (L3): Suggest pricing and scheduling from past ticketing and attendance. *Needs first:* Two or more seasons of linked ticketing and headcount data.

*Field note:* Free and community events rarely get counted properly, so reports show the ticketed audience only and the outreach work looks invisible.


## Stage: Documentation & Alumni

**An archive you can search, and alumni careers you can show**

Performance is ephemeral and the archive is often a hard drive in someone's drawer. Without rights, credits and dates attached, recordings become unusable. Alumni careers are the long-term evidence of what your support did, and they only show up if you ask regularly and link answers to the original grant.

*Decision it serves:* Lets the archivist and director decide what to preserve and share, and lets leadership show funders what happened to artists over five or ten years.

*How the work flows:*
1. **Collect final reports** (Programme officer): Receive grantee reports in text, audio or video, linked to the grant record.
1. **Catalogue assets** (Archivist): Tag photos, recordings and documents with artist, event, date, credits and rights.
1. **Clear rights** (Archivist, artist): Record usage permissions for web, print, research and funder reporting.
1. **Track alumni** (Alumni coordinator): Annual short survey on new works, commissions, awards, teaching, income from practice.
1. **Assess cultural impact** (Director, MEL): Combine alumni data, audience reach and critical response into an impact review.

*Where AI genuinely helps (MAP: Power):*
- Collect Faster (L2): Suggest metadata tags for photos and recordings: event, instruments, setting, visible text. *Needs first:* Assets linked to event records and a controlled tag list.
- Know Faster (L3): Pull alumni career updates from public sources to prompt follow-up. *Needs first:* Consent from alumni to track public mentions.
- Build Faster (L2): Draft cohort impact reports from alumni survey data and archive highlights. *Needs first:* Linked alumni and grant records.

*Field note:* Recordings get saved with file names like 'final_final_2.mp4' and no credits. Five years later, nobody knows who performed or whether you can use it.
